# Changelog

What changed in each release of Varians, for those who run it. The section of a release is the text of its release notes on GitHub, so each section begins with a heading `## <version>` and ends where the next begins. Versions follow semantic versioning; the data format has versions of its own, in [docs/formats](docs/formats/).

## Unreleased

### For operators

- **The dictionaries come as one file.** `varians lexicon` imports the lexicon published for the release (29 MB, checked against its SHA-256); `varians lexicon import FILE|URL [--sha256 HEX]` loads one exported elsewhere, `varians lexicon export FILE` writes the loaded one, and `varians lexicon build` builds it from its sources, the LSJ and the Middle Liddell of the Perseus Digital Library, the Ancient Greek Dependency Treebank and the analyses of Morpheus, downloading what it lacks from pinned commits. There is no `lexicon.sqlite` any more, and the command no longer takes a path or address of its own. [docs/lexicon.md](docs/lexicon.md) and [docs/install.md](docs/install.md) describe it.
- A dictionary lemma is now found whatever its case, so a proper name opens its entry directly.
- **Once after updating, run `varians cli conform-pages`** (after the usual backup) and keep what it prints. It puts every transcript's page breaks in the shape the workbench now expects: each names a page of the witness, once, in the order of the pages, without a label; the text of a page break that names no page goes to the text on no page. It prints one line for each transcript it changed, as a change in that transcript's history with the message "Pages reorganized", and nothing on a second run. It has no migration of its own.
- **An edition's conventions no longer have `latin`**, the setting for Latin abbreviations. An edition that still has it stored loads and prints as any other, and the member goes the next time its settings are saved; an archive or package that has it is read with it ignored, and none is written with it. No migration and no command are needed. The data format stays at version 2 (see its changelog).
- **Take a backup before updating: migration `0019_works_always_public` drops the `visibility` column of `works`, so every work that was private becomes public** (its title, author and reference scheme are then seen and listed by everyone). What is unfinished in it, a draft edition, a draft transcript, a conjecture or reported reading not yet published, stays visible only to those who may edit it. After updating, each work's archive is written again once, so that its `work.json` no longer says that a work was private. An archive or package written before still reads, and what it says of a work being private is ignored.
- **Take a backup before updating: migration `0018_minor_variants` changes data in place.** It lets `edition_site_kinds.kind` be empty, adds `minor`, and turns each row with `kind = 'unreported'` (a place left out of the apparatus, as an earlier build had it) into a minor variant with no kind. The importer still reads `kind: "unreported"` in an archive as minor.
- **Collations are recomputed once after updating**, by themselves, as the editors and the readers ask for segments: they are derived data, so there is no migration and no command is needed. The text is now collated in passages (see For editors), so the first opening of a long edition takes a little longer. Until the cache is filled, the reader of a long edition opens on the segments nearest the one asked for and takes in the rest as it arrives, as described below.
- **A paragraph break of an edition may carry `lineation`**, `verse` or `prose`, the kind of the paragraph it opens (absent, the kind of the edition's `conventions.lineation`), and the TEI writes it as `<lb/><lb type="prose"/>` or `<lb/><lb type="verse"/>`. Nothing in an archive written before changes its meaning. [docs/formats/specification.md](docs/formats/specification.md) and [docs/formats/tei-subset.md](docs/formats/tei-subset.md) say it exactly.
- **In an edition's TEI a segment begins at a milestone and encloses nothing.** The text is one block in which each segment begins with `<milestone unit="segment" n="…">`, as in a transcript's TEI, where there was an `<ab n="…">` around each segment: the TEI of every edition changes when its archive is next written, and a program that read the old shape must be changed. A word that a segment boundary divides is one word in the TEI, the start of the segment inside it marked `break="no"`, as a line break inside a word now is in an edition and a segment start inside a word in a transcript. Every variant is one `<app>` where its words stand, with the line breaks and segment starts between its words inside its lemma. The variant of a witness without a whole segment stands around the variants inside that segment, which were left out of the file before. A segment the edition does not print has its label. The header declares that the text is cited by its milestones and that the apparatus is by parallel segmentation. [docs/formats/tei-subset.md](docs/formats/tei-subset.md) has the rules, and [docs/formats/CHANGELOG.md](docs/formats/CHANGELOG.md) lists the changes.
- **The archive's files of an edition have its conjectures and reported readings**: the TEI, the plain text and the Markdown give those the archive holds, as the reader's downloads give those a reader sees. In the TEI, the header lists the scholars who proposed the conjectures and the sources stated for conjectures and reported readings, and each reading points at its entry (`resp`, `source`), where it had the name and the statement as text; a conjecture of the edition itself points at the edition's editors, and a reading reported for a witness names the witness in `wit`, as a collated reading does. The TEI has the edition's bibliography too, in the archive and in the reader's download: the works cited as structured entries (`<biblStruct>`), and what a conjecture or a reported reading cites of them, with the place cited, among the sources its reading points at. The text of a segment in the plain text and the Markdown no longer ends in an empty line.
- **The plain text with apparatus gives a segment the edition does not print**, as words added, under that segment's label.

### For editors

- **Every paragraph of an edition is verse or prose.** The edition's setting says which its paragraphs are to begin with, and the **Paragraph** menu at the top right of the text, beside **Insert**, sets the paragraph at the caret or those a selection touches: **Verse**, **Prose**, **New Paragraph**. The kind is a mark on the paragraph break that opens the paragraph. In verse the lines are shown as the editor breaks them, whatever the segments, and Enter breaks the line; inside a word it divides the word, and joining the lines gives the word back whole. In prose the text runs on to the width of the page and breaks at paragraphs, and Enter begins a new paragraph; the line breaks stay in the text and show again if the paragraph is marked as verse. The reader, the editor and the history show a paragraph alike, with the label of each segment in the run of prose.
- **A word divided between two lines or two segments is one word.** Each part is in the segment it stands in, the word has one normalized form and is compared whole. The hyphen is never typed and never stored: it is drawn where a line that is shown ends inside the word, so a prose paragraph has none. An edition keeps the words its witness divides; a transcript's text is as before.
- **The text is collated in passages, and a variant may lie across the boundary of two segments.** A passage is one segment or several taken together where something may cross the boundary between them: a word divided across it, words that a witness has at it and the edition does not, a segment that a witness is without, or a witness that departs from the edition close to it on both sides. A variant across a boundary is labelled by both segments (_1.5–1.6_), and its link goes to the first. Words that a witness merely assigns to the neighbouring segment make no variant. A word that a witness has in one place and is without in another is two variants that lead to each other, also across passages. In the editor's band a variant stands under the segment it begins in, and a line in the section of the segment its words lie in says _See 1.1–1.2_; **Next Variant**, **Previous Variant**, **Next to Review**, the count, the kind, **Minor** and **Keep the Text** follow it there. Assigning the same words to segments otherwise changes the labels of variants and nothing else, but for a word that stands twice: where the edition has the same word on either side of a boundary and a witness has it once (or the reverse), so that the letters cannot say which of the two the witness has, it has the one of the segment its own transcript gives the word to.
- **A segment adopted into the text follows the paragraph.** Words adopted where the edition had none take the segment their witness gives them; where that is a segment the edition did not have, they stand on a line of their own in a verse paragraph and in the run of the text in a prose one.
- **The history of an edition runs a prose paragraph on**, with the label of each segment in the run and no hyphen in a divided word; a line break that a change touched is marked where it stood. A word that the end of a line of verse divides has its hyphen drawn there.
- **The interface says _segment_ where a segment is meant**: the reading setting is **Segment Labels** (_As in the edition_, _Every segment_), and the guide no longer calls a segment a line. A line is a line as shown, as broken, or of a manuscript.
- **A dialog never grows taller than the window.** Its title and buttons stay in view and what is between them scrolls; before, the buttons of a long dialog could fall below the edge of a small screen.
- **Next Variant and Previous Variant start from the caret.** With the caret on a word that has no variant, they went to the first or last variant of the whole text; they now go to the nearest variant from the segment the caret is in.
- **A variant can be marked minor.** **Minor**, in the apparatus band, is a toggle that replaces **Leave out** and **Report**. It is separate from the kind (textual, interpretative), which a variant keeps when it is marked minor. A minor variant stays in the band, set apart; it is no longer counted among the variants left to review, and its words are not dotted in the editor's text. The plain text among an archive's files leaves it out, and the TEI has it as `<app type="textual|interpretative" subtype="minor">`. Readers are sent minor variants: the reading settings (the gear) have **Minor Variants**, _Shown_ or _Not Shown_, not shown to begin with. A published version keeps the kinds and the minor variants as they were when it was published, and the archive's `siteKinds` entry is `{ kind?, minor? }`. A variant left out in an earlier build is minor now (see For operators).
- **A siglum can stand for several witnesses.** **Witnesses and Sigla…** takes **Groups**: a siglum for at least two witnesses of the conspectus, with a note if wanted ("consensus codicum VH"). A variant lists the group under its siglum with its members beside it where all of them have the reading. A group follows its witnesses through every change of the conspectus (a siglum changed is changed in the group; a witness taken out leaves it; a group left with one witness is removed), and the reader's conspectus lists the groups.
- **A speaker or heading that opens a line is exported with that line.** In the plain text and the TEI of an edition it stood at the end of the line above.
- **Each variant is over its own words.** Every witness is now compared with the edition by itself, so the order of the sigla no longer changes the variants, and a witness that agrees with the edition is never reported as differing. Variants that stand next to each other are separate. What others read otherwise inside a stretch that a witness does not have is a variant of its own. A reading in words over a phrase (a transposition, one phrase for another) is one variant that says what every witness has for the phrase. Review marks on variants whose extent has changed are gone: those variants count as not yet looked at.
- **A variant speaks only of the witnesses that are there.** A witness whose transcript has a gap over a variant's stretch (text lost, illegible or not transcribed) is in none of its readings, and a gap alone makes no variant. A witness that has lost any word of the stretch is not there for that variant; for the variant over a whole segment, having part of its words counts as having it. A witness whose text runs on without the words is there, and the words are _not in_ it: nothing is called an omission, and a loss is never inferred from how much is missing, so mark a gap where the carrier is lost. A witness that has lost a whole segment, or whose transcript does not reach it, has no text for it: the reader's Witnesses panel lists such segments under _no text for_ and the editor's band says _No text here_. The print apparatus, the plain text and the TEI no longer say _lac._, _deest in_ or _om._ of a loss, and the TEI has no reading that is a loss.
- **A stretch can be marked as not transcribed.** **Insert**, **Not Transcribed** in the workbench puts a mark at the caret for a stretch the transcriber leaves out. It shows in italics as _not transcribed_ in the workbench and as (not transcribed) in the reader and in the witness pane of the edition editor, prints so in the plain text, is `<gap reason="ellipsis">` in the TEI, and keeps its reason through TEI, Leiden+ and the archive. Collation treats the witness as not there.
- **Pages hold their text.** In the workbench each page of the witness has its own text, typed on that page; there are no page breaks to make. Text on no page has a place of its own, **Not on a page**, where imports without pages land; **Move to 12r** in the toolbar under a selection puts text on its page, proposing the page the facsimile shows, and cut and paste moves words with their features and segments. **Whole Text** shows everything, read only, a page under each label.
- **Removing a page never deletes text**: its text goes to Not on a page in every transcript of the witness, and the dialog says how many words that is. Moving a page moves its text with it.
- **Segments are independent of pages**: moving text never changes a word's segment, and a page's first line shows in grey the segment it continues.
- **The workbench is arranged for transcribing**: the page, the form, Insert and Tools above the text, the whole-transcript actions in the header, **Segment…** in the selection toolbar, and **Facsimile on the Left** in the ⋯ menu. A transcript opens on the page last shown, and a witness with neither pages nor text opens on its pages.
- **Imports land where they belong**: a TEI file's pages go to the pages of the witness that have their labels, and the rest of the text to Not on a page or to the page shown, as chosen.
- **An edition with many witnesses opens and saves much sooner.** The editor opens on the first segments and counts the variants of the whole text a moment later, and the apparatus of the segments around the caret comes as the caret reaches them; the reader is sent the variants of the whole text at once, and the words of each witness at a variant when its panels need them; where the apparatus has to be made afresh (after an update that changes how it is collated) the reader opens on the segments nearest the one asked for and takes in the apparatus of the others as it arrives. An edition of thirteen hundred lines and thirty witnesses opens in about a second or less where it took fifteen, a save of one word takes a fraction of what it did, and the apparatus, the plain text and the archive are made in a fraction of the time. Nothing is shown otherwise than before.
- **A segment that witnesses have and the edition does not print is a variant.** It is a variant at a point, after the segment before it in the work's numbering: _not in_ with the witnesses whose text runs on without it, then the segment's words with the witnesses that have it, named an addition. Only segments between the first and the last that the edition prints are reported. In the edition editor a small mark shows where the segment would stand (in verse a caret in the margin between two lines, in prose a point in the text after the last word of the segment before it), the band lists it at its place, and **Next Variant**, **Previous Variant** and **Next to Review** reach it. Adopting one of its readings prints the segment after the segment it follows, its words taken from the witness chosen and in that segment, and **Undo Adoption** takes it back; **Keep the Text** leaves it unprinted and reviewed. Adopting the absence of a whole segment and then adopting the segment back gives it again; a first or a last segment removed in this way is no longer reported.
- **The order of whole segments is a variant.** Where witnesses have segments in another order, the variant is over the segments concerned, with a row of the labels in each order and the witnesses that have it; a block far from its place is given by the segment it follows. Readers see it: in verse as a bracket in the margin, in prose on the labels of the segments. Before, only the editor was told, and it cannot yet be marked minor or reviewed. Comparing the order of segments is much quicker on long editions.
- **The reader's variant window is a list.** A word with a dotted line opens the label and the edition's words, then _In 7 of 9 witnesses:_ with their sigla (or _In all 9 witnesses_), _Addition in_ with the witnesses where the words are an addition (the edition does not print them and witnesses have them, or the edition prints them as deleted), and one row for each reading with the sigla of its witnesses, the edition's first and in bold, _not in_ for witnesses without the words, a group as its siglum with its members in brackets. Every siglum opens that witness in the Witnesses panel with the words lit. Reported readings and conjectures follow with their sources and references, _minor_ and _interpretative_ show in the head, and the editor's notes on the words come last. Where the edition has no words and no witness agrees, the first row reads _not in the edition_. A word that a witness has in another place nearby is two variants, each row leading to the other, and is not called an addition. The Variants panel lists the same for the segments in view.
- **The edition editor's band lists each variant as the reader's window does.** The edition's words head it, and clicking them brings the text to them; then _In 7 of 9 witnesses:_ with their sigla (or _In all 9 witnesses_), _Addition in_ where the words are an addition, and one row for each reading with the sigla of its witnesses, the edition's first and in bold, _not in_ for witnesses whose text runs on without the words, a group as its siglum with its members in brackets. Reported readings and conjectures follow with their proposers, sources and references, and an adopted conjecture stands in the edition's row, marked _Adopted_. A reading is adopted by clicking its words in its row, where before the readings stood in a line after colons, and Alt+1 to Alt+9 adopt the rows in the order the band lists them. A siglum shows that witness in the Witnesses tab beside the text. The order of whole segments is a variant in the band, at the first of its segments, with a row of the labels in each order and nothing to adopt (**Line ↑** and **Line ↓** move lines); it replaces the sentence that said where a witness has a segment (_A has it after 1.8_). The band says no _omitted_ or _added_ and uses no Latin abbreviation.
- **Adopting that a whole segment is not in the text takes its line break with it** where it stood on a line of its own. The segment goes out of the text entirely, where before its words went and an empty line stayed. It is then a segment the edition does not print, and adopting its reading there prints it again, once.
- **A conjecture on some words of a segment is placed on those words and never on the variant over the whole segment.** Where a witness was without the whole segment, a conjecture on a word that the witnesses agree about was placed on the variant over the whole segment, and adopting it replaced the segment. It now stands at its words, at the occurrence it was made on where they stand more than once in the segment; one whose words are no longer in the text is listed at the foot of the segment.
- **Variants are marked where they stand.** Where the edition has no words and a witness has some, a small caret stands in the space between two words; the neighbouring word is no longer underlined for it. Variants over whole segments are marked by the kind of the paragraph they are shown in. In verse they are marked in the margin, between the labels and the words: a rule along a segment that some witnesses do not have (its words are no longer dotted for it), a caret between two lines for a segment the edition does not print, a bracket along segments in another order. In prose they are marked in the text like any other variant: the words of a segment that some witnesses do not have are dotted, a segment the edition does not print has the small caret after the word before it, and segments in another order have their labels dotted; a stretch that runs over both kinds is marked in each by that paragraph's kind. Each mark opens the variant window, takes the Tab key, is hidden in print, and follows the reading setting **Mark words with variants**.
- **No reading is labelled by its grammar or its sense.** The reader no longer says "another form of the same word", and the editor's band no longer tags readings _form_, _word_ or _order_. The analyser of Greek forms serves the dictionary and the **Normalize Diacritics** tool, and no apparatus.
- **The edition editor and the reader show the page where a witness's words stand**, whether the words are mapped to the image or not; arrows in the editor browse the witness's pages.
- **A witness is deleted from its own page** (⋯, **Delete Witness…**), where the menu says why when it cannot be.
- **Leiden+ comes in and goes out.** The import's Leiden tab reads Leiden+, the notation of papyri.info, with everything it can say of a papyrus, and **Download Leiden+** in the workbench's ⋯ menu writes a transcript in it. TEI and EpiDoc imports read the same things: numbers with their values, glyphs, paragraphoi and rules, words in another language, quotations, symbols with their expansion, what is said of a gap, and the doubt of an editor.
- **The import knows a text to start from from a transcript of the witness.** A text to start from (an earlier edition from the web, plain text) gives its words and their numbering and nothing else: none of its editor's brackets, notes, speakers or headings, and not its pages, which are that edition's; nothing is recorded of where it came from. A transcript of the witness made elsewhere (TEI or EpiDoc, Leiden notation, PAGE or ALTO) is read with its features and its pages, and its source can be recorded. A TEI file may be either, and the import asks which. Until now every TEI import took the file's page breaks for pages of the witness and its editor's marks for features of the witness.
- **A transcript no longer takes in what an editor supplied.** A transcript that is imported leaves out letters restored in square brackets and keeps the gap, and the witness is not there at it; a word broken by a gap counts with it. Corrections of the scribe's spelling become normalized forms, to be confirmed. Transcripts imported before are not changed.
- **A scribe's correction of a word is read both ways**: imported as a substitution, it is two words, the one deleted and the one added, so that an edition can collate the witness before and after correction.
- **A transcript keeps the shape of its pages whatever happens meanwhile.** Text typed on a page that someone removed in the meantime, a page begun by two people at once, or the undoing of a page's removal no longer leaves a page break without its page: the save puts the text in order, the status line says so, and the History shows it as _Pages reorganized_.
- **Importing and pulling no longer let a word slip into another segment.** Changes made here and on another installation to different words could together carry a word into a segment neither side gave it (one side moving a word to the head of a line, the other changing the segment that begins before it). Such a text is now reported as changed on both sides, for you to keep yours or take theirs. The same holds where both sides put words at the very start of a text: each installation would have set the other's words first, and the two would have differed for good.

- **An edition is made on one page, Make New Edition**, on the front page, on Your Work and on a work's page: the name of the edition, the work, the witnesses with their sigla and transcripts, and the editors. The works and the witnesses of the registry are each listed on the page under a search field, with **Choose**; several witnesses are chosen one after another. A work, a witness or a transcript that is not there yet is made in a window over the page (**Add New Work**, **Add New Witness**, **Make New Transcript**) and saved with its window. Only the work and the name are needed. The page asks for the **Name of Edition**, where the form said Title, and no longer for a base transcript, a range of segments or conventions.
- **Saving keeps you on the page, which becomes Edition Settings.** It holds what the editor's Edition Settings window held, with one **Save** for all of it, and **Read** and **Edit Text** beside the edition's name; **Edition Settings** in the editor's **⋯** menu leads there. A witness of the conspectus may be without a transcript, and any transcript of it that you may use can be chosen, whether or not it has text of the work yet.
- **An edition starts without text, and text is copied into it from the transcripts.** **Insert**, **Copy Text from Transcript…** in the editor, which an edition without text also offers where its text would be, copies a range of segments from any transcript of the conspectus, as often as wanted: from one transcript for some segments, from another for others. Segments the edition has are replaced where they stand, after the window has said how many; the others go after the segment that precedes them in the work. Each copy is one change in the edition's history. No transcript is an edition's base any more.
- **Every work is public.** A work is a catalogue entry (author, title, reference scheme): everyone sees and lists it, and there is no private work and no Visibility setting where a work is added or edited. What is unfinished and your own is a transcript, an edition, a conjecture or a reported reading: it stays a draft, seen only by you and those who may edit it, until you publish it. Look for a work before adding it, so as not to make a duplicate. A witness's page lists a work, and counts its transcripts and readings, only by what you may see of them.
- **The print apparatus is not offered in the interface**, a printed edition being work for later. **Edition Settings** has no section for it: its form, where the interpretative variants are printed, Latin abbreviations and the words for the other witnesses are not asked for, and what an edition has stored of them is left as it is. The plain text with apparatus is not among an edition's downloads (the TEI XML and the package are); its address still answers, and an archive still has the file, which says its words in English (_not in_, _added_, _deleted_).
- **An edition's name opens the reader everywhere**, also under Continue on the front page, on Your Work and in the account's ownership offers, where it opened the editor. **Read**, **Edit** and the **⋯** menu stand beside it on every card and row of an edition you may edit, and in the reader, the editor and Edition Settings. The menu has the same items in the same order everywhere, and every item works from a list: **Edition Settings**, **Publish…**, **Unpublish…**, **Make a Copy…**, the downloads, **Delete Edition…**.
- **A work, a witness and a transcript have one ⋯ menu beside their name**, on their own pages and in every list, as an edition has. For a work: Other Installations, the package, Merge into…, Delete Work…; for a witness: Merge into…, Delete Witness…; merging and deleting have left the Edit pages. A transcript's menu is the same in the workbench, on a witness's and a work's page, on Your Work and under Continue: Settings…, Make a Copy…, TEI XML, Leiden+, Delete Transcript…; its name is one of its settings, and **Import…** and **Number Lines from Here…** are under **Tools**. On a work's page **Make New Edition** and **Make New Transcript…** stand in the headings of their sections.
- **Conjectures, reported readings and bibliography entries are edited and deleted from their rows**, and a reported reading from the witness's page as well.
- **A refusal to delete a work, a witness or a transcript names the editions you may see and counts the others**, so that the name and owner of another person's draft are told to nobody.
- **Notes, maintainers, other installations, tokens and pages are removed only after Varians' own window has asked.**
- **Publishing, unpublishing, deleting an edition and removing a collaborator ask in Varians' own window**, named after the action, where the browser's own box asked.
- **Collaborators and the offer of ownership are under Owner and Collaborators on Edition Settings**; the editor's Collaborators… and Offer Ownership… are gone. **Witnesses and Sigla…** is a button in the editor's Witnesses pane. The editor's Preview is now **Read**, and Edition Settings' Edit Text is **Edit**.
- **What one clicks and every heading is in Title Case**, and field labels, messages and hints in sentence case. Adding a work, adding a witness, making an edition and making a transcript go by one name each wherever they are offered: **Add New Work**, **Add New Witness**, **Make New Edition**, **Make New Transcript**, with **Save** to complete them. A button or menu item that opens a window ends in an ellipsis.
- Signed in, the front page lists the published editions under **Editions**, as it does for visitors, and has no **Read Edition** button.

### For readers

- **The reading settings choose whether interpretative variants are shown**, as they do for minor variants: **Interpretative Variants**, _Shown_ or _Not Shown_, shown to begin with. The choices of showing a print apparatus below or beside the text, and of its form, are gone: a reader who had chosen them gets the page without an apparatus, and one who had chosen not to see interpretative variants still does not. Printing the reader's page gives the text with its labels and preface, and no apparatus.
- **The reader's ⋯ menu is the edition's menu**: **Make a Copy…**, signed in, and the downloads, the TEI XML and the package. The published versions are in a menu of their own, **Versions**.
- **Copying a draft one may not see answers "not found"**, like every other address of such a draft.
- **The front page for visitors** says what Varians is and lists the published editions under the heading **Editions**, or says that there are none yet. The buttons it had are gone: **Log In** and **Register** are in the header.

## 0.2.0

The first public release.

### For operators

- Two ways to install: Docker Compose (`compose.yaml`, with Caddy for certificates under the profile `tls`) and a native release tarball with a systemd unit. [docs/install.md](docs/install.md) is the operators' guide.
- `varians` (bin/varians), the operator's command: `serve` applies the migrations and starts the server; `migrate`, `backup`, `restore`, `cli`, `lexicon`, `version`.
- `GET /health` answers with the version and the state of the database, for health checks; `GET /api/version` with the version and the address of its source.
- Mail over SMTP (`SMTP_URL`, `MAIL_FROM`): addresses are confirmed at registration, passwords can be reset by their owner, and administrators can send a link to set a new password. Without mail, registration works as before, and an administrator makes reset links on the users page or with `varians cli reset-password <email>`.
- The footer shows the running version and links its source code.
- `lexicon:import` (`varians lexicon`) accepts an https address as well as a path, and checks a SHA-256 given with `--sha256`.
- Migrations 0012 to 0017 run on start: the archive's queue, persons and the parents of changesets, email tokens (the addresses of existing accounts count as confirmed), licences and rights of records and images, the end of the free-text licence columns (take a backup first), API tokens and remotes.
- New optional settings: `DATA_LICENCE` (CC-BY-SA-4.0 by default), `SITE_NAME`, `SECRET_KEY` (to keep tokens of other installations), `ARCHIVE_WRITE_DELAY_SECONDS`.

### For editors

- **The archive** of a work holds every record, follows every change within a minute, and is committed at every named moment and publication in the name of the person who made it.
- **The Varians Data Format, version 2**, is a specification with JSON Schemas, a TEI customization (ODD and RelaxNG) and an example, served by every installation at `/formats`. The TEI names the witness words each edition word comes from, the passages' CTS URNs, the licence and the people responsible.
- **Persons and ORCID iDs**: the history names people, who may give their ORCID iD on the account page; people from other installations are recorded without an account.
- **Imports never overwrite**: a text changed only on the other side is brought up to date, changes on both sides in different places are merged, and changes in the same words are reported, to keep yours or take theirs.
- **Typed identifiers** of works and witnesses (CTS URN, Diktyon, Trismegistos, Wikidata, VIAF, address), found by search, and **merging** of two works or two witnesses registered twice.
- **Every record has an address**, `/id/<id>`, leading to its page or, asked for JSON, giving the record as the archive holds it.
- **Licences and rights**: a published record carries the installation's data licence, fixed at its first publication; every image has its rights statement and credit line, and only images whose statement allows it are copied into archives and packages; the reader says how to cite an edition. **Terms of contribution** are accepted before editing or publishing.
- **Packages**: works, editions, transcripts and the whole installation download as checked packages (BagIt with RO-Crate metadata); **Import…** previews a package before bringing it in.
- **Other installations**: a work's maintainers compare, pull from and push to its copy on another installation, with tokens made on the account page.
