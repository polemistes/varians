# The TEI subset

What Varians reads from and writes to TEI P5 (`packages/core/src/tei/`). Everything in the tables has a place in the stream model; elements outside them are read through (their text kept) with a warning, and never written.

**The ODD is normative.** What Varians writes is defined by the TEI customization [`varians.odd`](varians.odd) and the RelaxNG schema generated from it, [`varians.rng`](varians.rng) (see [BUILD.md](BUILD.md)); every `transcription.tei.xml` and `edition.tei.xml` must be valid against it. The customization is a pure restriction of TEI P5 4.12.0: a document valid against it is valid against tei_all. This page is the readable account of the same subset, and says how each element maps to the stream; where the two disagree, the ODD is right and this page is to be corrected. The reading side is wider than the writing side: the importer accepts any TEI and keeps what it can.

## Structure

## What a transcript keeps of an edition

A TEI document is read in one of two ways, chosen by whoever imports it (`importTei`, option `as`).

As **a text to start from** (`as: 'text'`) it gives its words and their numbering and nothing else: the word it prints where it gives a choice (the regularized, corrected or expanded one), its lines and paragraphs, and the numbered structure for the segments. Its marks, gaps, notes, figures and paratext are left behind, and its `<pb>` and `<cb>` are passed over as if not there: they are the pages of its own edition, not of the witness to be transcribed.

As **a transcript of the witness** (`as: 'transcript'`, the default) it is read with its features and its pages, as the rest of this document describes. A transcript holds its witness and nothing an editor added to it, so the elements by which the source's editor supplied or struck out are not kept as marks:

| TEI                                                        | In the stream                                                                                                                                                                                           |
| ---------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `<supplied reason="lost">`, and with `evidence="parallel"` | left out; the gap it fills is kept, as long as its letters: a gap object where whole words were supplied, a point mark `gap` in a word that keeps other letters. A `<gap>` inside it is counted with it |
| `<supplied reason="omitted">`                              | inside a word, letters of its normalized form only (`unchecked`); a whole word is left out                                                                                                              |
| `<surplus>`                                                | inside a word, letters of its diplomatic form only (`unchecked`); a whole word stays, unmarked                                                                                                          |
| `<choice><sic/><corr/></choice>`                           | the sic is the diplomatic form, the corr the normalized form (`unchecked`)                                                                                                                              |
| `<app type="editorial">`, `<app type="alternative">`       | the `<lem>`: the reading the source prints                                                                                                                                                              |
| `<num value/>` without letters, `<gap reason="omitted">`   | left out                                                                                                                                                                                                |

What Varians itself wrote is read back with `as: 'verbatim'`, which keeps every element as its mark, the marks `supplied`, `surplus` and `corr` of the table below among them; they belong to editions' texts.

A `<gap>` that touches the letters of a word, before, inside or after them, is a point mark of that word; one that touches none is an object. Collation does not read a word with such a mark: it counts with the gap.

## Leiden+

[Leiden+](https://papyri.info/docs/leiden_plus), the notation of papyri.info, is read by writing the EpiDoc each of its constructs stands for and reading that (`leidenPlusToEpiDoc`, `importLeidenPlus`), and a transcript is written as Leiden+ by `toLeidenPlus`: lines with their numbers, pages and columns as divisions, and every feature it has a way of saying. Segments, boxes and variants noted in the margin have none and are left out.

| any other `<app><lem/><rdg/></app>` on import                                | the lemma's words only, with a warning                                                                    | editions are written with `<app>`, see below                                                                                             |

A **correction** is a `del` or `add` mark (TEI `<del>`, `<add>`). Its `attrs.hand` names the hand that made it: a hand of the witness by id, written as `hand="#hand-ID"` pointing at the witness's `handNote`; `scribe` (the main hand, written as its handNote when one is registered, else left unsaid); `other` (an unidentified hand, left unsaid); or a value as imported, written as it came. Its `attrs.seq` is the stage of correction (TEI `@seq`): 1 for the first layer, 2 for the next; corrections without a stage belong to no particular layer. An edition can collate a witness as first written, after all corrections, after a stage, or with the corrections of one hand.

A **variant noted in the carrier** (a reading written in the margin or between the lines, often introduced by γρ., or elsewhere on the page) is the mark `variant` on each token of the words it concerns, all with the same `attrs.group`: `{ kind: 'variant', attrs: { group: 'k3f9', reading: 'ἔννεπε μοι', place: 'margin', hand: 'h2' } }`. It is always a whole-token mark, and a token has at most one. The span is written as one `<app>` whose `<lem>` holds the words as the text has them (with any breaks and marks inside) and whose single `<rdg>` holds the variant; the `<rdg>`'s `type` is the place. An edition may collate the variants as a text of their own under a sub-siglum (`A v.l.`): the words of each span are replaced by the reading, the rest of the witness as corrected.

## Writing

A transcription's text is written in `<body>` as one `<ab>`, or one `<p>` per paragraph when the stream has paragraph breaks, with breaks as `<lb/>`, `<cb/>` and `<pb/>` (`break="no"` after a token joined to the next; a text that ends in a joined token ends with `<lb break="no"/>`, which is read as the join and not as a line break, and a line break after that token is a second `<lb/>`), the start of each labelled segment as `<milestone unit="segment" n="LABEL"/>` (with `break="no"` where it begins inside a word: it does not end the word, as TEI says of a break or a milestone there), and, in the archive, every token as `<w xml:id="w-ID">` with `facs` pointing at its zones and a normalized form that differs as `<choice><orig>…</orig><reg>…</reg></choice>`. A token of a word in several tokens says which part of the word it is, `part="I"` the first, `M` one in the middle, `F` the last. Marks are written as the elements of the table above, nested where their ranges nest and split where they overlap; a mark kind without an element of its own (`crux`, a note over a range) is `<seg type="KIND">`. Attributes of the XML namespace that an imported feature had (`xml:lang`) are written back with their prefix.

TEI allows no `<head>`, `<speaker>`, `<trailer>` or similar block inside `<ab>` or `<p>`, so paratext of those kinds is written as `<label type="head">`, `<label type="speaker">` and so on, the paratext's own type as `subtype`; `<label>`, `<stage>` and `<fw>` stand as themselves with the paratext's type as `type`. The importer reads both forms back to the same object.

## Header

`<titleStmt>` with the title, and in an edition an `<editor xml:id="editor-N">` for each editor; `<publicationStmt>` with `<authority>Varians</authority><availability><licence>` when the record has a licence, else a plain statement; `<encodingDesc>` with `<refsDecl><refState unit="segment"/></refsDecl>`, which declares by TEI's milestone method that the text is cited by its segments, the label of each being the `n` of the `<milestone unit="segment">` that begins it, in an edition `<variantEncoding method="parallel-segmentation" location="internal"/>`, and in the archive the prefixes of the record pointers; `<profileDesc><langUsage>` with a `<language ident>` for every language the words have, and `xml:lang` on `<text>`, the language most of the words have (the first of them where two have as many); a `<w>` whose word is in another language carries its own `xml:lang`, and without word elements such a word is written as `<w xml:lang>` all the same, so that its language is not lost. In `<sourceDesc>`, a transcription has `<msDesc>` and an edition `<listWit>`, and either may have `<bibl type="sources">` with the sources its maker states; a source description with nothing to say has a `<p>`. An edition whose apparatus names scholars or sources has besides a `<listBibl>` of them, at which the readings point, and an edition that cites works of the bibliography a second `<listBibl>` of those works (see Editions).

`<msDesc>`: `msIdentifier` (settlement, repository, idno; when none of these is known, `msName` with the witness's name, or its siglum, since TEI's rule `msId_minimal` forbids an identifier that is empty or begins with the siglum; then `altIdentifier type="siglum"`), `head` (name), `msContents/summary` (description), `physDesc/objectDesc` with `form` codex, scroll or leaf and `supportDesc` (material, extent), `handDesc hands` with one `handNote` per hand (`xml:id="hand-ID"`, `n` the label, `scope` sole, major or minor; the scribe of the text is sole or major), `history/origin/origDate`.

## Facsimile

Written: `<facsimile><surface xml:id n><graphic url width height/><zone xml:id ulx uly lrx lry|points/></surface></facsimile>`, with `<pb facs="#surface">` and `<w facs="#zone">` linking text to image. Coordinates are in pixels of the image when its size is known (`width` and `height` in `px`), else in thousandths of its width and height; a polygon, and a turned rectangle, are written as `points`. Zones are not read back from TEI yet; the archive's `zones.json` is the authoritative record.

## Editions

`exportEditionTei` writes the critical text with the parallel-segmentation apparatus, which the header declares: `<listWit>` from the conspectus (`<witness xml:id="wit-SIGLUM" n="SIGLUM">`, the siglum made a name: characters that cannot stand in one become `_`, and sigla that then coincide, such as A¹ and A², are told apart as `wit-A_` and `wit-A_-2`; no `<listWit>` when the conspectus is empty), the preface as `<front><div type="preface">`, and the text in `<body><div type="edition">` as one `<ab>`, in the order of the edition.

**A segment is an address and no container.** It begins with `<milestone unit="segment" n="label"/>`, as in a transcription, and runs to the next; nothing encloses it, so that a word, a line, a paragraph or a variant may lie across its start. In the archive the milestone has the segment's id and the passage's CTS URN where the segment first begins (`xml:id="seg-ID" corresp="urn:cts:…"`); a segment printed in two places begins twice, the second time with its label alone. A segment that begins inside a word has `break="no"`, as a line break there has: `gam<lb break="no"/><milestone unit="segment" n="1.2" break="no"/>ma` is one word, of which each part is in its segment. With the words written as `<w>`, each token of such a word says which part of it it is (`part`). The file has a line for each segment that begins between words of the text itself; inside a word and inside a variant a segment begins where it stands. Text of no segment is not written.

Paratext is written as in a transcription (a marginal note of the edition as `<label type="margin">`), and what opens a line before its first word (a speaker, a heading) stands after the start of the segment it opens. A line break is `<lb/>`, a paragraph break `<lb/><lb/>`, the second `<lb>` typed `verse` or `prose` where the paragraph has a kind of its own (`<lb/><lb type="prose"/>`), and none where it is of the edition's own convention; a paragraph break that opens the text stands before the first segment. Whether a break is shown (the lines of a prose paragraph are not) is for the reader of the stream; the TEI keeps every break. Variants noted in a witness's carrier belong to the transcription and are not written in the edition's text.

**A variant is one `<app>`, where its words stand**: `<app><lem wit="#wit-A #wit-C">…</lem><rdg wit="#wit-B">…</rdg></app>` (the base is left out of `wit`). The `<lem>` is written as the text outside an `<app>` is: whatever stands between its first word and its last stands inside it, the line breaks, the starts of segments and the objects. A variant across the boundary of two segments therefore has the start of the second in its lemma:

```xml
<milestone unit="segment" n="1.1"/>alpha beta gamma <app><lem wit="#wit-A">delta<lb/><milestone unit="segment" n="1.2"/>epsilon</lem><rdg wit="#wit-B">kappa lambda</rdg></app> zeta eta theta
```

- A reading of nothing is an empty `<rdg wit/>`, and a witness that has lost the words is named in neither `<lem>` nor `<rdg>`. A `<rdg>` has the witness's words; where the witness begins a segment that the edition prints is not said in it, and is in the witness's transcript.
- A variant at a point, where the edition has no words, has an empty `<lem/>` and stands where the words would: before the edition's word that follows. Between the last word of a segment and the first of the next, it stands directly after the last word, before the break that ends the line, where the witnesses' words are of the first segment, and after the start of the second where they are of that one.
- A segment that the edition does not print and some witness has is a variant at a point between the two segments around it, after what ends the first and before the start of the second. The reading begins with the segment's own milestone, which the file has nowhere else: `<app><lem wit="#wit-B"/><rdg wit="#wit-A"><milestone unit="segment" n="1.2"/>gamma delta</rdg></app>`.
- Where a witness is without one or more whole segments, the variant that says so stands around the other variants over the same words, which are found without that witness, an `<app>` inside its `<lem>`:

  ```xml
  <milestone unit="segment" n="1.2"/><app><lem wit="#wit-A #wit-B">gamma <app><lem wit="#wit-A">delta</lem><rdg wit="#wit-B">kappa</rdg></app> epsilon</lem><rdg wit="#wit-C"/></app>
  ```

  Should another variant lie across one of its ends, it is written in pieces, each around what it can hold and each with the empty `<rdg>`, since that the witness has nothing is true of every piece. The pieces are linked by TEI's global attributes: each has an `xml:id` that begins `app-` and is unique in the file, every piece but the last has `next="#…"` and every piece but the first `prev="#…"`; a conjecture or a reported reading has its words in the first piece and an empty `<rdg>` of the same `type`, `resp` and `source` in the others. No other variant has an `xml:id`.

- A conjecture is `<rdg type="conjecture" resp="#…" source="…">`, an adopted conjecture `<lem resp="#…" source="…">`, and a reading reported for a witness `<rdg type="reported" wit="#wit-SIGLUM" source="#…">`. The file a reader downloads has those a reader sees; the archive's file has those the archive holds, as they were when the version written was published.
  - `resp` and `source` are pointers, as TEI defines them, at the header's `<listBibl>` of the scholars and sources the apparatus names. `resp` points at who proposed the conjecture: the `<author>` of the scholar's entry, `<bibl><author xml:id="resp-Bentley">Bentley</author></bibl>`, the name made a name of the file's own as a siglum is, and two scholars whose names then coincide told apart by a number. A conjecture of the edition itself, which names no proposer, points at the edition's `<editor>` elements, and has no `resp` where the edition names no editor.
  - `source` points at the source stated for the record, `<bibl xml:id="src-1">Allen's apparatus (1931)</bibl>`, a source stated for several records being one entry. In the archive, where the header declares the prefixes, the `source` of a conjecture names its record first: `source="conj:<id> #src-1"`.
  - A work of the bibliography that the record cites is a source too, written as the record cites it: what is said before the work, a `<ref>` to the work under the label it is cited by, and the place cited as `<citedRange>`, `<bibl xml:id="src-2">cf. <ref target="#bib-ID">West 1998</ref>, <citedRange>45</citedRange></bibl>`. The sources of a record stand in `source` in this order: its record, the source stated, the works cited in their order.
  - A reading reported for a witness names the witness in `wit`, as a reading collated does. A witness known to the edition only by such a reading, and not in its conspectus, is listed in `<listWit>` after the conspectus, by its siglum.
- A place where the witnesses have the same letters read otherwise is `<app type="interpretative">`. A place the editors have marked as minor is written like any other, with its kind and the grade beside it, `<app type="textual" subtype="minor">` or `<app type="interpretative" subtype="minor">` (a `subtype` stands only beside a `type`, so the type is always given then); an untyped `<app>` is textual and not minor.
- A variant over a lacuna of the edition has the `<gap>` in its lemma.

**The works cited.** The works an edition cites, in its notes, of itself and in the conjectures and reported readings of its apparatus, are a second `<listBibl>` of the header, headed _Works cited_, in the order of their labels. Each is a `<biblStruct xml:id="bib-ID" type="TYPE" n="LABEL">`, TEI's structured entry, made from the biblatex entry: `type` is the entry type and `n` the label the edition cites it by (_West 1998_, with _a_ and _b_ where two would read alike).

- What is published by itself (a book, a thesis, a report) is a `<monogr>`: its `<author>` and `<editor>` elements (a translator is an `<editor role="translator">`), its `<title level="m">` (`level="u"` for a thesis and for what is unpublished) with the subtitle after a colon, `<idno type="ISBN">`, `<idno type="ISSN">` and `<idno type="DOI">`, the `<edition>`, and the `<imprint>` with `<pubPlace>`, `<publisher>` (the institution of a thesis) and `<date>`; a volume follows as `<biblScope unit="volume">`, and a series as `<series>` with its `<title level="s">` and the number in it.
- What stands in a journal or in a book is an `<analytic>`, with its authors, its `<title level="a">` and its DOI, before the `<monogr>` it stands in: the journal, `<title level="j">`, with volume, issue and pages as `<biblScope>` and the date in the `<imprint>`; or the book, with its editors, `<title level="m">`, imprint, and the pages after it.
- A name with a given name is `<forename>` and `<surname>`; any other (an ancient author, a body) stands as it is. A date that is a year, a month or a day has it as `when`. Pages that are a range have `from` and `to`. An address is `<ptr target>`.
- Only what the entry says is written. Where TEI asks for what the entry lacks, the element is there and empty: `<imprint><date/></imprint>`, and a `<title>` after a name.

```xml
<biblStruct xml:id="bib-01J…" type="article" n="Wilamowitz 1927"><analytic><author><forename>Ulrich von</forename> <surname>Wilamowitz-Moellendorff</surname></author><title level="a">Lesefrüchte</title></analytic><monogr><title level="j">Hermes</title><imprint><biblScope unit="volume">62</biblScope><biblScope unit="page" from="276" to="298">276–298</biblScope><date when="1927">1927</date></imprint></monogr></biblStruct>
```

## Restrictions of the ODD

Beyond the choice of elements, the ODD closes the values of a few attributes that have a fixed meaning here: `milestone/@unit` is `segment` (or `undefined`, for a sign on the carrier that numbers nothing); `lb/@type` is `verse` or `prose` (the kind of the paragraph that begins at a paragraph break of an edition); `app/@type` is `textual` or `interpretative` and `app/@subtype` is `minor`; `rdg/@type` is `margin`, `interlinear` or `other` (a variant noted in the carrier) or `conjecture` or `reported` (in an edition); `objectDesc/@form` is `codex`, `scroll` or `leaf`; `altIdentifier/@type` is `siglum`. It documents the prefixes `tok`, `seg`, `wit`, `conj` and `person` for `<prefixDef>` without closing the list. Other attributes are as TEI defines them, since imported features keep theirs.

## What is not in TEI

Attested separators (`sep`), the `unchecked` flag, segment run parts, review marks and the change history live only in the JSON files of the archive. So do an edition's notes on its words and segments, and what a note cites: the works are in the list of works cited, the note and its citation are not. The note a conjecture or a reported reading carries is in the JSON only too.
