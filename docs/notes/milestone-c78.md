# C7 (canonical identifiers) and C8 (merging registry entries): notes for PLAN-2 section 13

Built on 9 November 2026 by Claude Opus 5.5 in a worktree, for the lead to merge. The schema (`works.identifiers`, `witnesses.identifiers`, `registry_merges`, migration 0013) and the archive's part (writing `identifiers` and `mergedIds`, matching on import) were there already; nothing under `archive/` or `packages/db/drizzle/` was changed, and no migration was added.

## Decided while building

### Identifiers (C7)

- **Checking.** `cts` must be a CTS URN of a work (`isCtsWorkUrn` of the core); `wikidata` is `Q` and digits without a leading zero, and a lower-case `q` is made capital; `diktyon`, `trismegistos` and `viaf` are digits only; `url` is an http or https address without spaces; `other` is anything. Every value is trimmed and at most 300 characters; at most 20 identifiers per entry; a row with an empty value is dropped; the same scheme and value is kept once. The first wrong value is reported with the value itself ("abc: a VIAF number has digits only."). Pasted resolver addresses (a Wikidata or Pinakes URL) are not taken apart: they are refused for those schemes and can be given as `url`. In `apps/web/src/lib/registry/identifiers.ts`, pure, so that the form and the server share it.
- **The form** is a list of rows, each a select of the scheme and a value, sent as one JSON field `identifiers`, like the hands of a witness. It is on the forms for new works and witnesses as well as on the edit pages. A new row takes the scheme of the row above.
- **Resolvers** on the pages: Wikidata `https://www.wikidata.org/wiki/<Q>`, VIAF `https://viaf.org/viaf/<n>`, Trismegistos `https://www.trismegistos.org/text/<n>` (the texts database: the number of a papyrus or of a text), Diktyon `https://pinakes.irht.cnrs.fr/notices/cote/<n>/`, and a URL itself. A CTS URN and Other are shown without a link.
- **Search** (`identifier-search.ts`): the value equals the query, ignoring case and the query's surrounding spaces; a part of a value finds nothing. It applies to the registry search (search-before-create, the picker for a merge) and to the library's works and witnesses (the header's search, the Works and Witnesses pages, Make Edition, Transcribe Witness). Beside the typed identifiers it matches the work's primary CTS URN (`works.identifier`) and the witness's catalogue identifier (`witnesses.identifier`) exactly as well; they still match nothing by substring, as before.
- `works.identifier` stays the primary CTS URN, edited and checked as before (not checked as a URN, so that existing values keep saving).

### Merging (C8)

- **Where.** On the edit page of the entry (where Delete is), a section "Merge into another entry" with **Merge into…**, leading to `/works/<slug>/merge` or `/witnesses/<slug>/merge`. The page offers a search (a plain GET form, `?q=`, the registry search, so title, author, siglum, shelfmark or an identifier) and **Choose** (`?into=<slug>`) shows the preview; the merge is a POST to `?/merge` with the survivor's id, and leads to the survivor's page. The page is open to administrators and the maintainers of the entry; the preview says when the person does not also maintain the survivor, and offers no button then. A private work that the person may not see is not previewed.
- **Who.** Administrators, and anyone who maintains both entries as creator or maintainer (`canMergeEntries` in `authz.ts`). A site editor's right to edit anything does not reach merging, as it does not reach deleting. The merge functions check this themselves, like the deletions.
- **What the survivor keeps.** Its title or name, description, siglum, visibility, scheme and settings. It gains the identifiers it lacks; for works the merged work's primary CTS URN becomes the survivor's primary one when it had none, and otherwise goes into its list (as `cts`, or `other` when it is not a URN).
- **Witnesses.** Moved: transcriptions, reported readings, sigla (edition conspectus rows), maintainers with the date they became maintainers (the merged witness's creator too, since the creator counts as a maintainer), pages after the survivor's in their own order. Also: the hands the survivor lacks (by id), appended as not the scribe, because the corrections in the moved transcripts name their hands by id; and the witness id inside the bases of published edition moments, so that a published version still names its witness. Blocked by an edition whose conspectus names both witnesses, and by a page label both have (compared trimmed, case-sensitive).
- **Works.** Segments are matched by address: the values of the scheme's levels in order, trimmed, as strings. Moved for a matched segment: conjectures, reported readings, notes, citations, alignment constraints, site reviews and site kinds (where the survivor's segment already has a row for the same edition and site, that row wins), transposition sequences of conjectures, the segment starts in the texts and the derived index; collation cache rows of the editions of both works are dropped. Unmatched segments move to the survivor, labelled and sorted in the survivor's scheme. Then conjectures, reported readings, editions, the transcriptions chosen for the work and maintainers move, the merged work is removed, and its scheme too when no other work uses it. Blocked when the schemes' levels differ (keys or types; labels and separators may differ, the survivor's are used), and when a moved segment's label in the survivor's scheme is already another address's label there. The latter cannot arise from schemes Varians accepts; it guards against data made otherwise. Edition slugs are global, so no clash is possible and none is checked.
- **Segment ids in texts.** Moving a segment id inside a transcription's or an edition's stream is not a change of the text: the stored content is rewritten in place, its version stays, and no changeset is recorded. So that past and published versions, which are rebuilt from checkpoints and changesets, name the same segment, the ids are rewritten in the document's checkpoints and in the operations (and reverses) of its changesets too, by exact replacement of the old segment id; the changesets keep their ids, authors and times. This goes slightly beyond "rewrite the stored content": without it a published edition of the merged work would be rebuilt with segments that no longer exist. Documents rewritten: the transcriptions whose text is assigned to the merged work or that were chosen for it, and the merged work's editions. A transcription that once assigned text to the merged work and no longer does keeps the old ids in its history; a segment that is not found is passed over when the index is built, so this is harmless. When a stream now has two runs of one segment with the same part (it assigned text to both works at one address), the runs are numbered again in order of their parts, then of the stream. The process's cache of rebuilt past versions is cleared for the rewritten documents (`forgetPastVersions` in `documents/service.ts`).
- **The record.** One `registry_merges` row (old id, kind, survivor, the merged entry's title or name, who merged); earlier rows whose survivor was the merged entry are pointed at the new survivor in the same transaction, so a chain is one step long. The survivor's page lists the merged entries with their dates ("Merged into this entry").
- **Locking.** Both entries are locked `for update` in the order of their ids, so two merges of the same pair cannot deadlock; the preview is computed again inside the transaction and the merge refused when anything now blocks it.

## Deploy note

- No migration, no setting. Migration 0013 (from the lead's part) must be applied, as it brings the columns and the table.
- After a merge, the merged work's archive directory under the archive root is left where it is, with its git history, as for a deleted work: the archive queue passes over works that no longer exist. The survivor's archive is rewritten by the queue as usual and now holds the moved records. The lead may want the writer to remove or mark such directories; until then they are stale copies, harmless.
- The Playwright spec `apps/web/e2e/merge.spec.ts` (register two witnesses, give one a Diktyon number, find it by that number, merge the other into it) is written and not run here.
